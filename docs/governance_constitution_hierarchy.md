# Governance Constitution Hierarchy

## Purpose

This document defines the constitutional dependency
hierarchy for HumanOS governance architecture.

The goal is to:
- establish governance precedence ordering
- preserve constitutional coherence
- clarify foundational vs derived governance semantics
- prevent replay-era governance contradiction
- formalize architectural dependency structure

Governance hierarchy determines which constitutional
principles dominate during orchestration ambiguity,
conflict, escalation, and replay reconstruction.

## Constitutional Hierarchy Overview

| Constitutional Layer | Role | Dependency Level |
|---|---|---|
| structural legality | topology + constitutional constraints | foundational |
| governance invariants | canonical orchestration protections | foundational |
| contradiction arbitration | conflict precedence semantics | foundational |
| reevaluation governance | temporal reassessment semantics | derived |
| rehabilitation governance | bounded recovery progression | derived |
| escalation governance | containment progression semantics | derived |
| governance observability | telemetry + reporting integrity | support |
| replay governance | historical reconstruction semantics | deferred |
| adaptive governance mutation | self-modifying governance behavior | deferred |

## Foundational Constitutional Principles

The following governance principles are considered
constitutionally foundational and may not be bypassed
through adaptive recovery pressure:

- topology legality
- restriction precedence
- legitimacy evidence requirements
- reversibility preservation
- observability preservation
- contradiction arbitration legality
- reevaluation continuity

Derived governance systems must remain subordinate
to foundational legality constraints.

## Derived Governance Semantics

The following governance systems derive authority
from foundational constitutional protections:

- rehabilitation pacing
- escalation progression
- reevaluation scheduling
- governance persistence behavior
- stabilization progression
- authority restoration sequencing

Derived systems may not override foundational
constitutional protections.

## Constitutionally Forbidden Hierarchy Violations

The governance runtime must never:

- override topology legality through confidence recovery
- bypass restriction precedence during restoration
- treat persistence as legitimacy evidence
- suppress reevaluation opportunities permanently
- conceal governance degradation from observability layers
- permit replay reconstruction to violate foundational legality
- allow adaptive governance mutation to rewrite foundational principles

## Replay Governance Implications

Replay-oriented governance systems must remain
subordinate to foundational constitutional legality.

Replay reconstruction may:
- analyze governance history
- reconstruct causality
- replay orchestration evolution

Replay systems may not:
- reinterpret topology legality retroactively
- mutate foundational constitutional protections
- legitimize illegal governance transitions
- suppress constitutional auditability

## Architectural Discipline

The constitutional hierarchy exists to preserve
long-term governance stability as orchestration
complexity increases.

HumanOS governance evolution prioritizes:
- legality preservation
- bounded adaptation
- replay safety
- reversibility
- auditability
- constitutional coherence

over rapid autonomous governance expansion.
