# HumanOS Design Principles

Status: Living Document

Purpose

This document records enduring architectural and product principles that guide HumanOS design decisions across all system components.

Unlike Architecture Decision Records (ADRs), these principles are expected to remain stable across multiple implementation phases and feature areas.

---

# Principle 1

## Separation of Computational Evidence and Professional Judgment

HumanOS preserves a clear distinction between computational evidence and professional judgment.

Computational evidence generated by HumanOS and professional observations recorded by practitioners shall remain separate information sources unless explicitly governed by future architecture.

This distinction ensures that:

- HumanOS never silently converts professional observations into governed evidence.
- Professional judgment never silently overrides computational evidence.
- The provenance of every observation remains explicit.
- Future integrations between these information sources require explicit governance.

---

# Principle 2

## Transparent Evidence Provenance

Every governed evidence object shall preserve traceable provenance from its originating observations through all subsequent interpretations and runtime decisions.

---

# Principle 3

## Human-Centred Collaboration

HumanOS is designed to support professional decision-making rather than replace it.

The platform shall present computational evidence and professional expertise as complementary sources of insight while preserving the identity and provenance of each.

---

# Future Principles

Additional product principles will be added as HumanOS evolves.


Canonical Evidence Principle

HumanOS shall maintain a single governed representation of participant evidence.

Adaptive runtime components shall consume participant evidence exclusively through the governed participant summary.

Parallel evidence representations shall be avoided where they create divergent runtime behaviour.
