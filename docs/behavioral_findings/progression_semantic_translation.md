# Progression Semantic Translation

## Purpose

This document defines the semantic boundary between:

- internal HumanOS governance mechanics,
- and educator-facing progression interpretation.

The goal is to ensure that HumanOS communicates:

- progression conditions,
- pacing behavior,
- stabilization continuity,
- and recovery patterns,

WITHOUT exposing:

- infrastructure abstractions,
- governance mechanics,
- psychometric assumptions,
- or identity-level inference.


---

# Core Principle

HumanOS does NOT interpret identity.

HumanOS interprets progression conditions.

The system should describe:

- how progression responds to challenge,
- how recovery behaves longitudinally,
- and how support adaptation affects continuity.

The system should NOT claim:

- personality traits,
- cognitive disorders,
- emotional diagnoses,
- or permanent learner characteristics.


---

# Semantic Abstraction Layers

## Internal Infrastructure Layer

Used only inside governance systems.

Examples:

- governance mediation
- oscillation analysis
- stabilization confidence
- escalation pressure
- authority ceilings
- arbitration state


---

## Progression Interpretation Layer

Intermediate interpretation semantics.

Examples:

- pacing strain
- recovery consistency
- stabilization continuity
- overload persistence
- bounded recovery
- adaptive responsiveness


---

## Educator-Facing Layer

Human-readable progression meaning.

Examples:

- challenge increases may be occurring too quickly
- recovery patterns are becoming more consistent
- support adjustments appear partially effective
- sustained overload continues disrupting progression
- progression remains responsive to pacing adjustments


---

# Internal → External Translation Mapping

| Internal Governance Meaning | Educator-Facing Meaning |
|---|---|
| escalating instability | pacing strain increasing |
| prolonged critical instability | sustained overload disrupting progression |
| bounded oscillation | progression remains inconsistent but recoverable |
| rising stabilization confidence | recovery patterns becoming more consistent |
| repeated stabilization resets | recovery continuity remains fragile |
| adaptive governance mediation | support adjustments remain partially effective |
| degraded governance persistence | pacing adjustments may still be insufficient |
| stabilization persistence | recovery consistency strengthening over time |


---

# Narrative Design Principles

Educator-facing summaries should:

- read as coherent natural language,
- describe progression conditions,
- remain actionable,
- avoid infrastructure terminology,
- avoid psychometric labeling,
- avoid deterministic conclusions,
- and preserve uncertainty where appropriate.


---

# Good Examples

## Good

"The learner appeared to recover more consistently
when challenge escalation slowed and support pacing
remained stable."

## Good

"Sustained overload continued disrupting progression,
though recovery patterns improved when pacing adjustments
became more gradual."

## Good

"Progression remained responsive to support changes,
but stabilization continuity fluctuated under sustained pressure."


---

# Bad Examples

## Bad

"Governance mediation remained bounded and adaptive."

Reason:
Infrastructure language exposed directly.

## Bad

"The learner demonstrates emotional instability."

Reason:
Identity-level inference.

## Bad

"The child struggles with executive dysfunction."

Reason:
Psychometric interpretation unsupported by progression data.

## Bad

"The learner is resilient."

Reason:
Permanent trait attribution.

# Canonical Progression Narrative Archetypes

These archetypes define reusable longitudinal
progression narratives that HumanOS may synthesize
from governance behavior and stabilization patterns.

Importantly, these are NOT learner identities.

They are progression conditions observed over time.


---

## 1. Sustained Overload Narrative

Triggered by:

- prolonged escalating instability,
- destabilizing oscillation,
- low stabilization confidence,
- persistent critical governance pressure.

Educator-facing interpretation:

"Sustained overload continued disrupting progression,
and recovery patterns remained inconsistent despite
support adjustments."

Meaning:

- pacing escalation may be occurring too aggressively,
- stabilization continuity has not yet emerged,
- support adaptation may require slower progression pacing.


---

## 2. Cautious Stabilization Narrative

Triggered by:

- bounded oscillation,
- moderate stabilization confidence,
- intermittent stabilization streaks,
- degraded but adaptive governance.

Educator-facing interpretation:

"Recovery patterns began improving when pacing changes
became more gradual, though progression continuity
remained somewhat fragile under sustained pressure."

Meaning:

- stabilization is emerging,
- but recovery legitimacy remains incomplete,
- pacing consistency still matters significantly.


---

## 3. Strengthening Recovery Narrative

Triggered by:

- rising stabilization confidence,
- persistent stabilization streaks,
- bounded instability,
- adaptive pacing recovery.

Educator-facing interpretation:

"Recovery behavior became increasingly consistent over time,
particularly when challenge escalation remained gradual
and support pacing stayed stable."

Meaning:

- recovery continuity is strengthening,
- support adjustments appear effective,
- stabilization durability is emerging longitudinally.


---

## 4. Fragile Continuity Narrative

Triggered by:

- repeated stabilization resets,
- oscillatory recovery,
- fluctuating stabilization confidence,
- intermittent pacing strain.

Educator-facing interpretation:

"Progression remained responsive to support adjustments,
but recovery consistency fluctuated when challenge pressure
increased too rapidly."

Meaning:

- recovery remains possible,
- but stabilization continuity is still vulnerable,
- pacing escalation sensitivity remains elevated.


---

## 5. Adaptive Responsiveness Narrative

Triggered by:

- bounded instability,
- stable pacing adaptation,
- moderate-to-high stabilization confidence,
- low oscillatory disruption.

Educator-facing interpretation:

"Progression remained responsive to pacing adjustments,
and recovery continuity stayed relatively stable
throughout the learning cycle."

Meaning:

- adaptive pacing remains effective,
- overload pressure remains manageable,
- stabilization continuity appears sustainable.


---

# Archetype Constraint

These narratives must remain:

- longitudinal,
- condition-focused,
- non-diagnostic,
- non-identity-based,
- and progression-oriented.

The narratives describe:

"how progression currently behaves under adaptive conditions"

NOT:

"what kind of learner the participant is."


# Architectural Constraint

HumanOS progression interpretation must remain:

- longitudinal,
- interpretable,
- bounded,
- progression-oriented,
- and condition-focused.

The system should communicate:

"what progression conditions appear to help or disrupt continuity"

NOT:

"what kind of person the learner is."
