# Ethics & Interpretation Principles

## Patterns Describe Populations, Not Individuals

This system is designed to observe and summarize patterns that emerge across tasks
and sessions. These patterns are **population-level signals** and **session-scoped
descriptions**, not permanent traits or diagnoses of any individual.

Averages, distributions, and trends describe how groups behave under certain
conditions. They do not guarantee outcomes for any single person, nor do they define
identity, ability, intelligence, or personality.

Individual sessions may deviate from aggregate trends. Exceptions do not invalidate
patterns, and patterns do not override individual context.

## Session-Scoped Interpretation Only

All summaries produced by this system apply only to:
- the specific task,
- the specific session,
- and the conditions under which that session occurred.

They are **non-diagnostic**, **non-inferential**, and **non-predictive**.
They must not be used to label, rank, or judge individuals beyond the immediate
context of task interaction.

## Statistical Literacy Principle

Responding to population-level statistics with anecdotes is not disagreement —
it is a mismatch of analytical levels.

This system explicitly distinguishes between:
- population patterns,
- session summaries,
- and individual self-reflection.

Failure to separate these levels leads to misinterpretation and misuse.
This distinction is enforced in system design, UI language, and data access rules.

## Data Access & Privacy

Participants retain access only to their own session data.
No participant can view or infer another participant’s results.

Administrative access, where present, is restricted to:
- aggregated,
- anonymized,
- non-identifying data.

No permanent labels, scores, or traits are assigned to individuals.

## Design Commitment

This system is built with the explicit commitment that:
- patterns do not equal people,
- summaries do not define identity,
- and metrics exist to support reflection, not classification.

Any future extension of this system must preserve these principles.
