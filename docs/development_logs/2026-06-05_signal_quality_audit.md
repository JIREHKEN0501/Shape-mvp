# Signal Quality Audit

## Objective

Assess the quality and reliability of participant signals within the pilot dataset prior to progression and trajectory analysis.

## Dataset

* Participants analyzed: 98
* Qualified participants (20+ attempts): 41

## Audit Findings

### Hesitation Signal Coverage

Observed:

* 70 participants with non-zero hesitation
* 28 participants with zero hesitation

This indicates that hesitation is present and measurable across the majority of participants.

### Zero-Hesitation Participants

A subset of participants demonstrated zero recorded hesitation despite substantial attempt counts.

Possible explanations include:

* Rapid confident responding
* Participant familiarity with tasks
* Internal testing activity
* Measurement limitations
* Genuine low-latency decision making

These cases should not automatically be treated as invalid.

### Internal Testing Consideration

A proportion of zero-hesitation participants may have originated from beta testing or development activities where task answers were already known.

This possibility must be acknowledged when interpreting signal distributions.

### Dataset Suitability

The dataset contains:

* High performers
* Moderate performers
* Low performers
* High hesitation profiles
* Low hesitation profiles
* Multiple difficulty exposure patterns

This variability provides sufficient diversity for initial trajectory and progression analysis.

## Conclusion

The hesitation signal appears sufficiently populated for exploratory trajectory analysis.

The presence of zero-hesitation participants should be documented as a potential confounder but does not invalidate the dataset.

The pilot dataset is considered suitable for progression and trajectory validation work.
