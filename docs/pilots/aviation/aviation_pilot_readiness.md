# Aviation Pilot — Readiness Overview

---

## 1. Purpose

The Aviation Pilot evaluates whether session-scoped,
identity-agnostic task summaries can support training
and simulation environments without becoming
certification, diagnosis, or surveillance tools.

This pilot is intended to support:
- Training task design
- Simulation quality assessment
- Instructor-led review

It does not assess pilot fitness, readiness, or competence.

---

## 2. What This Pilot Is Not

This pilot is explicitly not designed to:
- Certify pilots or trainees
- Assess flight readiness or safety clearance
- Diagnose cognitive or psychological conditions
- Predict future performance under real flight conditions
- Replace instructor judgment or regulatory processes

All outputs are non-certifying and non-authoritative.

---

## 3. Participant & Consent Model

Participation requires explicit consent.

Participants must be informed that:
- The system analyzes task performance only
- No identity is stored by the platform
- No longitudinal internal tracking occurs
- Outputs are for training reflection only

Any linkage between session data and real individuals
is handled externally by the training organization.

---

## 4. Task Design Rules

Aviation pilot tasks must:
- Be simulation-based or abstracted training tasks
- Measure observable task interaction only
- Avoid real-world flight decision authority
- Be session-scoped and independently evaluable

Tasks must not:
- Simulate live flight decision authority
- Infer readiness, fitness, or risk
- Reference past sessions internally

---

## 5. Summary Outputs

Summaries describe task interaction only.

They may include:
- Accuracy within simulated tasks
- Timing and response variability
- Task-specific interaction metrics

They must not include:
- Safety judgments
- Readiness indicators
- Certification language
- Predictive claims

---

## 6. Human Interpretation

All interpretation is instructor-led.

The system does not:
- Recommend actions
- Flag safety concerns
- Escalate results automatically

Instructors remain responsible for interpretation
within existing aviation training frameworks.

---

## 7. Aggregation & Reporting

Aggregation may occur only at:
- Task level
- Cohort level

The system does not:
- Track individual progress internally
- Support automated progression decisions

---

## 8. Risk & Misuse Mitigation

Risks include:
- Over-interpreting summaries as readiness signals
- Using outputs for certification decisions

Mitigations include:
- Strict language constraints
- Documentation clarity
- Structural enforcement of session scope

---

## 9. Success Criteria

The pilot is successful if:
- Instructors find summaries useful for task design
- No outputs are used for certification or clearance
- Participants report trust and clarity of purpose

Safety is preserved by limitation, not expansion.
