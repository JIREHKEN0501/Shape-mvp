# HumanOS Evaluator Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric defines the standardized evaluation criteria used by independent human evaluators during HumanOS progression validation studies.

The objective of this rubric is to provide a consistent framework for interpreting participant progression trajectories and to reduce evaluator subjectivity.

HumanOS validation depends upon comparing system-generated interpretations against independent human assessments.

For this comparison to be meaningful, evaluators must apply the same definitions when reviewing participant trajectories.

This rubric therefore establishes the operational meaning of progression categories used throughout HumanOS validation.

---

# Evaluation Philosophy

Evaluators should assess observable progression patterns rather than attempting to infer permanent characteristics about participants.

The objective is not to determine:

* Intelligence
* Talent
* Personality
* Motivation
* Future potential

The objective is to determine whether observable task interaction patterns indicate meaningful progression states.

All evaluations should remain grounded in observable evidence.

---

# Evaluation Inputs

Evaluators may review:

* Accuracy trajectories
* Difficulty progression
* Latency trajectories
* Retry behavior
* Hesitation behavior
* Recovery patterns
* Session summaries
* Participant task histories

Evaluators should not have access to HumanOS interpretations during assessment.

This requirement prevents confirmation bias and preserves evaluator independence.

---

# Primary Classification Rule

Evaluators should identify the single progression category that best describes the dominant participant trajectory across the observation window.

The goal is not to describe every aspect of participant behavior.

The goal is to identify the most representative progression pattern.

If multiple categories appear plausible, evaluators should select the category supported by the strongest overall evidence.

---

# Improving

## Definition

A participant demonstrates a meaningful positive trajectory across the observation period.

Performance indicators generally improve over time.

Improvements may include:

* Increased accuracy
* Improved consistency
* Reduced hesitation
* Reduced retries
* Improved performance at higher difficulty levels
* Faster recovery from challenge

Improvement does not require perfect performance.

Improvement requires observable positive movement.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Accuracy increasing across tasks.
* Stable accuracy despite increasing difficulty.
* Decreasing latency without loss of accuracy.
* Reduced retry frequency over time.
* Increased success under challenge.

## Classification Guidance

Temporary setbacks do not invalidate an improving classification if the dominant trajectory remains positive.

---

# Stable

## Definition

Participant performance remains relatively consistent throughout the observation period.

Neither meaningful improvement nor meaningful deterioration is observed.

The participant demonstrates behavioral consistency without significant directional movement.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Similar accuracy throughout the observation window.
* Consistent latency.
* Stable retry behavior.
* Stable hesitation levels.
* Consistent performance across difficulty levels.

## Classification Guidance

Stability should not be confused with plateauing.

Stable participants show no clear evidence that improvement has slowed following earlier gains.

The dominant observation is consistency.

---

# Plateauing

## Definition

The participant demonstrates evidence of prior improvement that subsequently slows, stalls, or ceases.

Performance remains relatively stable despite continued participation or exposure to challenge.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Early gains followed by sustained flattening.
* Difficulty progression without corresponding performance gains.
* Repeated performance at the same capability level.
* Stable but non-improving outcomes despite continued activity.

## Classification Guidance

Plateauing requires evidence of a prior upward trajectory.

Without evidence of prior improvement, evaluators should generally classify the participant as Stable rather than Plateauing.

---

# Recovering

## Definition

The participant demonstrates observable improvement following a period of deterioration, instability, or reduced performance.

Recovery represents a positive trajectory emerging from a previously degraded state.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Accuracy improves following a decline.
* Retry behavior decreases after a period of escalation.
* Performance stabilizes after instability.
* Latency normalizes following deterioration.
* Success rates improve after a sustained period of struggle.

## Classification Guidance

Recovery requires evidence of a prior negative phase.

Without a preceding deterioration period, evaluators should generally classify positive movement as Improving rather than Recovering.

---

# Regressing

## Definition

The participant demonstrates a sustained negative trajectory across the observation period.

Performance indicators worsen over time.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Decreasing accuracy.
* Increasing retries.
* Increasing hesitation.
* Failure under previously manageable difficulty.
* Declining consistency.

## Classification Guidance

Isolated poor performances should not automatically produce a Regressing classification.

The negative trajectory should represent the dominant pattern.

---

# Inconsistent

## Definition

Participant behavior exhibits substantial fluctuation without a clearly dominant progression pattern.

Performance alternates between improvement, deterioration, and recovery in a manner that prevents reliable classification into another category.

## Indicators

Examples include:

* Large swings in accuracy.
* Highly variable latency.
* Alternating periods of improvement and decline.
* Unstable response to challenge.
* Repeated reversals of trajectory.

## Classification Guidance

Inconsistent should be used only when no single progression category clearly dominates the observation window.

---

# Secondary Confidence Assessment

After selecting a progression category, evaluators should assign a confidence level.

### High Confidence

Strong evidence supports the selected classification.

Alternative classifications appear unlikely.

### Moderate Confidence

Evidence generally supports the classification but reasonable alternatives exist.

### Low Confidence

Evidence is ambiguous or insufficient.

Multiple classifications appear plausible.

Confidence ratings will support future calibration analysis and disagreement investigation.

---

# Disagreement Analysis

Evaluator disagreement should not automatically be treated as evaluator error.

Disagreement may indicate:

* Ambiguous participant trajectories.
* Weak signal quality.
* Insufficient observation windows.
* Category definition weaknesses.
* HumanOS interpretation limitations.

Disagreement should therefore be treated as a valuable source of insight during validation.

---

# Relationship To HumanOS Validation

This rubric provides the human reference framework used throughout HumanOS progression validation.

HumanOS outputs and evaluator classifications will be compared using these standardized definitions.

The purpose of the comparison is not to determine whether humans or HumanOS are universally correct.

The purpose is to determine whether HumanOS interpretations correspond to observable progression patterns identified independently by trained evaluators.

This rubric therefore serves as the foundation for future validation studies, confidence calibration efforts, signal attribution analysis, and eventual Bayesian modeling initiatives.
