# HumanOS Evaluator Observation Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric defines the observation framework used during HumanOS validation studies. Its purpose is to provide independent evaluators with a standardized method for reviewing participant trajectory artifacts and recording observations.

The rubric is designed to evaluate observable trajectory characteristics only. It is not intended to assess intelligence, personality, emotional state, psychological conditions, diagnoses, or permanent participant traits.

The objective is to determine whether HumanOS trajectory interpretations align with observations made independently by trained evaluators reviewing the same evidence.

---

## Validation Principle

HumanOS is a trajectory estimation system rather than a participant classification system.

Evaluators must therefore focus on observable behavioral patterns within the provided observation window.

All observations should be interpreted as session-scoped findings and not as statements about the participant outside the observed context.

Patterns describe observed trajectories, not individuals.

---

## Evaluation Materials

Each evaluator will receive:

* Participant trajectory artifact
* Observation window summary
* Difficulty exposure summary
* Accuracy summary
* Latency summary
* Hesitation summary
* Recovery observations (when available)
* Adaptation observations (when available)

Evaluators will not receive HumanOS interpretations prior to scoring.

This maintains blind evaluation conditions.

---

## Observation Category 1: Performance Stability

Definition:

The degree to which participant performance remains consistent throughout the observation period.

Evaluation Guidance:

High stability is characterized by relatively consistent performance with limited fluctuation.

Low stability is characterized by significant fluctuations or repeated performance collapse and recovery cycles.

Scoring:

1 = Very unstable

2 = Unstable

3 = Moderately stable

4 = Stable

5 = Highly stable

---

## Observation Category 2: Challenge Retention

Definition:

The degree to which performance is maintained as task difficulty increases.

Evaluation Guidance:

High challenge retention is observed when performance remains relatively strong despite exposure to increased challenge levels.

Low challenge retention is observed when performance deteriorates substantially as challenge increases.

Scoring:

1 = Very poor retention

2 = Poor retention

3 = Moderate retention

4 = Strong retention

5 = Exceptional retention

---

## Observation Category 3: Observed Friction

Definition:

The degree of observable effort reflected by latency, hesitation, retries, and related telemetry signals.

Evaluation Guidance:

High friction indicates substantial observable effort or difficulty navigating tasks.

Low friction indicates relatively smooth progression through tasks.

Scoring:

1 = Minimal friction

2 = Low friction

3 = Moderate friction

4 = High friction

5 = Very high friction

---

## Observation Category 4: Adaptation Response

Definition:

The observable relationship between challenge exposure and participant performance.

Evaluation Guidance:

Evaluators should assess whether the participant appears to maintain performance under changing conditions or demonstrates difficulty adapting to changing challenge levels.

Scoring:

1 = Very poor adaptation response

2 = Poor adaptation response

3 = Moderate adaptation response

4 = Strong adaptation response

5 = Exceptional adaptation response

---

## Observation Category 5: Trajectory Direction

Definition:

The overall direction of observable performance throughout the observation window.

Evaluation Guidance:

This category evaluates whether evidence suggests improvement, stability, decline, or inconsistency.

Scoring:

1 = Strong decline

2 = Moderate decline

3 = Stable trajectory

4 = Moderate improvement

5 = Strong improvement

---

## Confidence Rating

After completing all observation categories, evaluators must provide a confidence assessment.

Confidence reflects evaluator certainty in the observations made from the available evidence.

Scoring:

1 = Very low confidence

2 = Low confidence

3 = Moderate confidence

4 = High confidence

5 = Very high confidence

---

## Qualitative Notes

Evaluators may record brief qualitative observations explaining their reasoning.

Qualitative notes must remain tied to observable evidence.

Acceptable examples include:

* Performance remained stable despite increased challenge exposure.
* Hesitation increased during higher difficulty tasks.
* Accuracy remained high throughout the observation window.

Unacceptable examples include:

* Participant appears highly intelligent.
* Participant is anxious.
* Participant lacks motivation.
* Participant has a learning difficulty.

Qualitative observations must describe evidence, not personal attributes.

---

## Agreement Analysis

Following completion of evaluator scoring, HumanOS trajectory interpretations will be compared against evaluator observations.

Agreement analysis will focus on:

* Performance Stability
* Challenge Retention
* Observed Friction
* Adaptation Response
* Trajectory Direction

Disagreement analysis will be used to identify interpretation weaknesses, signal weighting issues, attribution failures, and areas requiring calibration.

The purpose of validation is not to prove HumanOS correct, but to measure the degree of agreement between HumanOS observations and independent evaluator observations under controlled conditions.

---

## Validation Constraint

HumanOS outputs and evaluator observations are restricted to trajectory-level interpretation.

Neither HumanOS nor evaluators may infer permanent participant characteristics from observation-window telemetry.

All conclusions are limited to observed behavior within the available evidence window.
