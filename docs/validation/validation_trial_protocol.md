# HumanOS Validation Trial Protocol

## Purpose

This protocol defines the procedure for executing the first HumanOS validation trial.

The objective of the trial is to determine whether HumanOS trajectory interpretations demonstrate meaningful agreement with observations made independently by human evaluators reviewing the same evidence.

The trial is not intended to validate participant outcomes, intelligence, personality, psychological characteristics, or permanent traits. The trial evaluates agreement regarding observable trajectory characteristics only.

---

## Trial Objectives

The first validation trial seeks to answer the following questions:

1. Can independent evaluators consistently identify observable trajectory characteristics from reconstructed participant artifacts?

2. Do HumanOS trajectory interpretations align with evaluator observations at a rate greater than chance?

3. Which trajectory dimensions demonstrate the strongest agreement between HumanOS and human evaluators?

4. Where do disagreements occur and what signals contribute to those disagreements?

The trial is exploratory and intended to validate the evaluation process itself before larger validation studies are conducted.

---

## Participant Selection

Validation Trial #1 will utilize participants drawn from the qualified pilot cohort.

Eligibility requirements:

* Minimum of 20 task attempts.
* Complete trajectory reconstruction available.
* Sufficient telemetry quality for analysis.
* Exclusion of known calibration, debugging, or test-only sessions when identifiable.

Initial trial size:

* Five participants.

Participant selection should include a mix of:

* High observed performance trajectories.
* Moderate observed performance trajectories.
* Lower observed performance trajectories.
* Varying hesitation profiles.
* Varying difficulty exposure profiles.

The objective is to maximize observable variation within the trial sample.

---

## Trajectory Artifact Preparation

For each selected participant:

1. Generate a trajectory reconstruction artifact.
2. Remove any HumanOS interpretation outputs.
3. Retain only observable evidence summaries.
4. Assign anonymized evaluation identifiers.

Evaluators must not know:

* Participant identifiers.
* HumanOS conclusions.
* HumanOS scores.
* HumanOS confidence estimates.

This maintains blind evaluation conditions.

---

## Evaluator Procedure

Each evaluator will receive:

* Trajectory artifact.
* Evaluator Observation Rubric.
* Scoring instructions.

Evaluators will independently assess:

* Performance Stability.
* Challenge Retention.
* Observed Friction.
* Adaptation Response.
* Trajectory Direction.
* Confidence Rating.

Evaluators may provide qualitative observations where appropriate.

Evaluators must base all judgments on observable evidence contained within the trajectory artifact.

No assumptions regarding participant characteristics are permitted.

---

## HumanOS Assessment Procedure

HumanOS will independently generate trajectory interpretations using the same participant telemetry used to construct the trajectory artifact.

HumanOS outputs will be recorded prior to evaluator review.

HumanOS outputs must remain hidden from evaluators until scoring is complete.

---

## Agreement Analysis

Following completion of evaluator scoring:

1. Collect evaluator scores.
2. Collect HumanOS scores.
3. Compare observations across each rubric category.
4. Identify agreement and disagreement patterns.
5. Record evaluator confidence levels.
6. Record HumanOS confidence estimates when available.

Agreement analysis will focus on:

* Directional agreement.
* Category-level agreement.
* Confidence alignment.
* Areas of persistent disagreement.

The purpose of agreement analysis is to identify strengths and weaknesses in HumanOS interpretation logic rather than to prove HumanOS correct.

---

## Disagreement Review

For each disagreement:

1. Identify the rubric category involved.
2. Review supporting telemetry signals.
3. Review trajectory reconstruction evidence.
4. Determine whether disagreement originated from:

   * Signal limitations.
   * Interpretation logic.
   * Evaluator inconsistency.
   * Missing evidence.
   * Ambiguous trajectory patterns.

Disagreements will be documented and used to improve future validation studies.

---

## Trial Success Criteria

Validation Trial #1 will be considered successful if:

* Evaluators can complete the rubric consistently.
* Trajectory artifacts provide sufficient evidence for evaluation.
* Agreement analysis can be completed without procedural issues.
* HumanOS outputs can be meaningfully compared against evaluator observations.
* Major interpretation disagreements can be traced to identifiable causes.

The objective of Trial #1 is process validation rather than statistical validation.

---

## Trial Limitations

The first validation trial is exploratory.

Limitations include:

* Small participant sample size.
* Pilot dataset constraints.
* Known retry-signal limitations.
* Potential hesitation-signal ambiguity within portions of the pilot dataset.
* Limited evaluator pool.

Results from Trial #1 should be used to refine methodology and prepare larger validation studies.

---

## Guiding Principle

Validation exists to measure agreement between HumanOS observations and independent human observations.

The purpose of validation is not to confirm HumanOS assumptions, but to identify where HumanOS observations align with evidence and where further calibration is required.
